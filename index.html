01 — Business Context

Recotap helps B2B marketing teams identify, target, and convert high-value accounts.

Recotap is an Account-Based Marketing (ABM) platform that gives enterprise marketing teams a unified view of their target accounts — combining intent data from G2, Bombora, and website visits with CRM signals to orchestrate personalized campaigns across display, LinkedIn, and email channels.

When I joined in December 2023, the product was functional but deeply problematic. It had been built quickly by engineers, with no dedicated design function. The UI was dark, dated, and disjointed — and the core campaign creation workflow was so confusing that customers needed hand-holding from the sales team just to launch their first campaign. This was killing activation and directly impacting revenue.

02 — Problem Statement

The platform felt like it was built for engineers, not marketers.

Enterprise marketers using Recotap were sophisticated, data-driven professionals managing six-figure ad budgets. But the product they were handed made them feel incompetent. The campaign creation flow — the most critical workflow in the entire product — was a four-step wizard that told users almost nothing about what they were doing or why.

What was broken

  • Dark, heavy navigation — visually dated, enterprise-hostile
  • Campaign wizard had 4 steps with almost no content per step
  • No contextual guidance — users didn't know what segments, targeting, or budgets meant in this context
  • No real-time feedback — users couldn't predict campaign outcomes before launching
  • Data was siloed — intent signals, engagement, and revenue impact were all in separate, unconnected views
  • Zero visual hierarchy — everything looked equally important

What needed to happen

  • Modernize the entire visual language — light, clean, data-forward
  • Rebuild campaign creation as a single, informed, confidence-building experience
  • Add real-time performance prediction so users could set expectations before launching
  • Unify data views — give marketers a complete picture of account engagement in one place
  • Build a scalable design system from scratch
  • Reduce handoff friction with engineering through documented components
03 — Before: The Old Interface

A wizard that created more questions than it answered.

The original campaign creation flow used a dark sidebar navigation and a stepped wizard model. Each step showed almost no content — the "Audience" step had just two fields, and the "Message" step dropped users into a raw template gallery with zero guidance. The result was a product that looked unfinished and felt intimidating.

Before
After

The transformation is not just visual. The old UI had 2–3 form fields per step across 4 screens. The redesign consolidates everything into a single, scrollable canvas — Schedule, Segment, Target Criteria, Budget, Ads, and Advanced Settings — while a persistent right panel shows Account Summary and live Performance Prediction (estimated reach, clicks, impressions) that updates as the user builds the campaign.

04 — Design Process

Research-informed. Constraint-aware. Shipped iteratively.

As the sole designer on the product, I owned the entire design process — from initial research through to dev handoff. The approach was grounded in understanding how enterprise marketers actually think about ABM, not just what the engineering team had already built.

01

Discover

Heuristic audit of existing product. CTO interviews. Study of competitor ABM platforms (Demandbase, 6sense, Terminus).

02

Define

Identified critical user flows: campaign creation, account monitoring, performance reporting. Prioritized by activation impact.

03

Design

Rebuilt navigation, campaign flow, dashboard, and account detail. Designed component library in parallel with features.

04

Deliver

Shipped in agile sprints with engineering. Iterated based on internal testing and early customer feedback.

05 — Redesign: Campaign Creation

From a confusing wizard to a confident, single-canvas experience.

The biggest design decision was abandoning the stepped wizard model entirely. Enterprise marketers don't think in linear steps — they need to see the whole picture at once. The redesigned campaign configuration page puts everything on one canvas with smart sectioning.

The persistent right-hand panel was a key innovation: it shows Account Summary (how many accounts are targeted, how many match the criteria) and Performance Prediction (estimated audience reach, approximate clicks, approximate impressions) that updates live as the user adjusts targeting and budget. This gave marketers confidence before they hit Activate.

06 — Redesign: Dashboards

Data that tells a story, not just displays numbers.

The dashboard was rebuilt from the ground up. I designed two distinct dashboard views: ABM Activity (campaign performance, journey stage funnel, top ads) and Revenue Impact (pipeline influenced, revenue influenced, media spend, avg spend per account). Each was designed to answer a different question — one for the campaign manager, one for the VP of Marketing.

Main Dashboard — unified view of ICP accounts, intent signals, and account performance

07 — Redesign: Account Intelligence

Everything a sales team needs to know about a target account, in one view.

The Account Detail page was designed to be the command center for a target account. It surfaces engagement timeline, contact-level engagement by department and seniority, G2 intent, website intent, Bombora intent, firmographic data, CRM deals, and top performing ads — all in a single, scrollable view with a persistent right panel for firmographics and quick actions.

This was one of the most complex information architecture challenges in the product. The page needed to serve both the marketing team (intent signals, engagement) and the sales team (deals, contact data, notes) without feeling overwhelming. The solution was a tabbed structure (Overview, Contacts, Engagement, Intent Signals) with the most critical data surfaced in the overview.

Account Detail — full intelligence view for a target account with engagement, intent, and CRM data
08 — Design System

Built a complete design system from scratch. Alone.

With no existing design infrastructure, I built Recotap's complete design system in Figma — covering color tokens, surface tokens, typography tokens, icon sets, third-party logos and integration icons, buttons, inputs, chart components, alerts, and data visualization components. The system was documented with usage guidelines and handed off to engineering with developer-ready specifications.

The design system was not a side project — it was built in parallel with product features, growing iteratively as new components were needed. This approach reduced design-to-dev handoff time significantly, as engineers could reference component specs directly rather than interpreting designs.

10 — Learnings

What I'd do differently, and what I'd do the same.

Build the design system earlier

I built components in parallel with features, which sometimes meant retrofitting. Starting with a foundational token system before designing screens would have saved time in later sprints.

Being the sole designer is a superpower

No design by committee. Every decision was mine to make and justify. This forced clarity of thinking and made the product visually and experientially coherent.

Enterprise users need prediction, not just configuration

The live performance prediction panel was the highest-impact single design decision. Users don't just want to set parameters — they want to know if those parameters will work.

The wizard pattern fails complex workflows

Breaking campaign creation into 4 linear steps forced users to commit to decisions without seeing the full picture. A single canvas with smart grouping was the right call for this level of complexity.

04 — Systems

I treat design systems as product infrastructure — not a style guide. Every component I build is designed to scale, documented for engineers, and rooted in real usage patterns.

My approach to design systems is token-first and usage-driven. I start with a color system and surface tokens, then build typography scales before touching any components. Every component is built to handle edge cases — empty states, loading states, error states — not just the happy path.

At Recotap, I built a system that covered: color tokens (primary, neutral, semantic), surface tokens for dark/light contexts, typography tokens (display through label), a complete icon set, integration logos for 50+ platforms, button variants, input types, chart components (line, bar, donut, gauge), and alert components.

The system was the backbone that allowed me to ship features faster as the product matured — and gave engineering a single source of truth for implementation.
